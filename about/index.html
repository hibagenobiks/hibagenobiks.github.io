About

Hiba Ali

Neurogenetics researcher at the Department of Neurology, Universitätsklinikum, working on Parkinson’s disease, ALS and frontotemporal dementia.

Story

I study why some brains degenerate, starting from the genome. Neurodegenerative diseases like Parkinson’s disease, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) and frontotemporal dementia (FTD) share more biology than their names suggest, and genetics is one of the few places where we can see cause rather than consequence.

My work moves along that path. It starts with finding the disease-causing variants hidden in patient genomes, including in the awkward corners of the genome where standard analysis fails. It continues with turning genetic information into biomarkers that can stratify patients and follow disease. And it ends with the therapeutic side: understanding how RNA folds, so that antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) can be designed to reach the RNA they are meant to silence.

Before joining the Department of Neurology, I worked in Bengaluru on the whole-genome sequencing of Indian Parkinson’s disease patients. That project became one of the first sequencing-based resources for Parkinson’s genetics in India. It also left me convinced that who we sequence matters as much as how we sequence them.

I also write here about the practical side of neurogenomics: the reference genomes, aligners and pipeline choices that quietly decide whether a patient gets a diagnosis.

Neurogenetics · Department of Neurology, Universitätsklinikum

From the genome to the neuron, and back to the clinic.

I’m Hiba Ali, a researcher working on the genetics of Parkinson’s disease, ALS and frontotemporal dementia. I find disease-causing variants in patient genomes, look for genetic biomarkers that track disease, and study RNA structure to help design better antisense oligonucleotide therapies.

Research

What I work on

Four connected threads: what goes wrong in the genome, how to measure it, how to target it, and who gets studied in the first place.

01

Neurogenetics of neurodegeneration

Finding the variants that cause Parkinson’s disease, ALS and frontotemporal dementia in patient genomes, including the hard-to-sequence loci, structural variants and repeat expansions that standard pipelines tend to miss.

  • Parkinson’s · ALS · FTD
  • Whole-genome sequencing
  • Variant interpretation
02

Genetic biomarkers

Genetic and molecular markers that can stratify patients, explain why disease looks so different from person to person, and help pick the right people for the right clinical trial.

  • Patient stratification
  • Polygenic risk
  • Clinical genomics
03

RNA structure & antisense therapies

An antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) can only act where it can reach its target RNA. I study how RNA folds, so that ASOs can be designed against sites that are actually accessible.

  • RNA structure
  • ASO design
  • Therapeutic targeting
04

Genomics across populations

Most of what we know about neurogenetics comes from European cohorts. My work on whole-genome sequencing of Indian Parkinson’s patients showed how much is missed when other populations are left out.

  • Population diversity
  • Diagnostic yield
  • Reference genomes
